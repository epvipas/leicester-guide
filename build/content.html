📜 A short history of Leicester

Roman Ratae. Leicester began as Ratae Corieltauvorum, the Roman capital of the local Corieltauvi tribe. The Jewry Wall, the surviving wall of its 2nd-century public baths, is one of the largest pieces of standing Roman masonry in Britain.

Earls and parliaments. In the Middle Ages Leicester was the seat of powerful earls. Simon de Montfort, Earl of Leicester, called the 1265 parliament that first included elected town representatives, and the city's university and a statue on the Clock Tower bear his name. Cardinal Wolsey died at Leicester Abbey in 1530.

The king in the car park. Richard III was killed at Bosworth in 1485 and buried in the Greyfriars church in Leicester, which vanished after the Reformation. In 2012 archaeologists found his skeleton under a council car park. DNA from living relatives confirmed it, and he was reburied in Leicester Cathedral in 2015.

Hosiery and boots. From the 1700s Leicester made stockings and knitwear, then boots and shoes. By the 1930s it was said to be one of the richest cities in Europe.

Today Leicester is one of the most diverse cities in Britain. Families from India and East Africa built the Golden Mile from the 1970s, and its Diwali celebrations are among the biggest outside India. Leicester City won the Premier League in 2016 against odds of 5,000 to 1.

👑 The king in the car park

In 1485 Richard III, the last Plantagenet king, was killed at the Battle of Bosworth, 15 miles west of Leicester. His body was brought back, displayed, then buried with little ceremony in the church of the Greyfriars. After the friary was demolished, the grave was lost for centuries.

In 2012 a dig led by the University of Leicester, prompted by the Richard III Society, found a skeleton with a curved spine and battle wounds under a council car park. DNA from two living descendants of his sister confirmed his identity. On 26 March 2015 he was reburied in Leicester Cathedral, a few yards from where he was found.

🚶 Richard III trail

A compact walk of about a mile around the city centre, then a short drive to the battlefield.

🍛 Eating Leicester

  • Leicester has repeatedly been named England's curry capital. The Golden Mile on Belgrave Road is the place for vegetarian Gujarati food, chaat and sweet marts.
  • Red Leicester: the city's own crumbly orange cheese, coloured with annatto.
  • Walkers crisps are made in Leicester, and the Melton Mowbray pork pie and Stilton come from the county.
